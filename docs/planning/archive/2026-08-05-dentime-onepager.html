🎯 What it is, and what it isn't

It isA way to pick a night that works for friends spread across timezones. Movie night, raid night, D&D session, con meetup.
It is not a world clockEight of those already ship at $0.99–$4. The time scrubber is table stakes, not a feature.
It is not a "group scheduler"Work-coded phrase. Files it next to Doodle and Calendly. Nobody adopts a work tool for movie night — but people happily use a friend tool at work.
The gap it fillsNobody combines a persistent people roster with group polling, natively, for friend groups. Doodle went web-only in 2025. When2Meet is a 2008 grid. Every roster tool was sold to remote-work teams and most are dead.

🗂️ Two tabs. That's the whole app.

Tab 1 — Den (opens here)
Person rows: avatar, name, their local time, timezone.
Packs — collapsible groups, LocalWP-style, drag people between them.
Time Peek — a scrubber at the top. Drag it and every time updates live. Fully client-side, never touches the network.
Add via friend code DEN·XXXX-XXXX, or manually for people who don't use DenTime.
Block on any row.
Tab 2 — Meetups (the reason people download it)
Host proposes 2–5 time slots. App generates HOWL·XXXX-XXXX.
Host pastes that code into Discord, group chat, wherever the pack already talks. DenTime sends nothing anywhere.
Anyone with the code joins by entering it. Everyone sees the slots in their own local time.
RSVP yes / no / maybe. Host sees a grid of who's in on what.
No title field. A meetup shows as "Nathanial's meetup" plus its date range.
Why join-by-code instead of invites
A push invite is a channel someone can aim at you unasked, and friend codes get shared publicly in Discord and Twitch chat. Removing the push removes the abuse vector entirely — and it's how people already share Doodle links.

It also deleted a notification system and an invite state machine from the build.

🔒 Locked decisions

Each one has a reason. Open it before relitigating.

Two tabs, not three
"Find a Time" was Time Peek under a second name. Time Peek is a scrubber inside Den, not its own tab. One less concept to explain.
Design for friend groups, market to all three
Designing for friends + teams + clients at once makes every screen slightly wrong for everyone. Packs solve it for free — a pack named "Acme Corp" behaves identically to "the boys". One design language, three landing page sections.
A Vocabulary setting that swaps seven nouns
Settings → General → Den (default) / Team. Swaps Den→Team, Pack→Group, Host→Organizer, Meetup→Meeting, Friend code→Member code, Who's in→Attending, Make a plan→Schedule.

The voice does not change. Same verbs, buttons, errors, tone. A work user isn't put off by friendly copy — they're put off by screenshotting "your pack" into a client Slack channel. Fix the nouns, leave the voice.

Den mode is the only mode in App Store screenshots.
All-native SwiftUI, one Xcode project
Not React Native — react-native-macos doesn't support menu bar extras as a first-class surface. Not three Xcode projects — watchOS can't build standalone and iPad is a checkbox, not a target.

apps/apple sits outside Turborepo's build graph. Xcode builds it; Turborepo never touches it.
Moderation is handled by design, not a team
No free-text titles means display names are the only user-written text in the app. No push invites means nobody can reach you unasked. What's left: Block, a Report menu item that opens a prefilled email, rotate-my-friend-code, and a contact paragraph on the site.

Result: a reviewer finds a planning tool with a block button, not a social network.

⚠️ Universal Purchase — the one that costs money if you miss it One purchase must cover Mac, iPhone, iPad and Watch. That requires every platform target to share one bundle ID: com.mrdemonwolf.dentime. Ship Mac as .dentime and iOS as .dentime.mobile and they become two separate $9.99 products — your Mac buyers pay again on their phone. Trivial now, unfixable after launch. Turn on Family Sharing while you're in there.
What paid-up-front deletes from the build
No unlocks table, no entitlement checks, no StoreKit code, no Restore Purchases button (ownership follows the Apple ID), no paywall screen, no upgrade prompts. Most of guideline 3.1 stops applying.
The tradeoff, and the thing not to do
Tradeoff: everyone joining a meetup has to own the app. DenTime works inside a group that's all bought in, not across a group where one person owns it. Deliberate — simplicity over reach.

Don't: a free download that opens onto a paywall is a 2.1 rejection. Apple treats a non-functional free app as a demo. Paid means paid on the listing; there's no middle ground.

Why $9.99: menu bar clocks sell for $0.99–$4. This has a backend and a network behind it.

Storage — not a concern, but three rules keep it that way

Integer primary keys, not UUID textA UUID as TEXT is 36 bytes and repeats through every foreign key and index. friend_code and join_code stay the public identifiers. Roughly halves storage.
Purge meetups older than 90 daysCron-triggered Worker. Ever-growing event tables are how people hit D1's 10 GB per-database wall.
Watch rows-read, not bytesFree plan is 5M reads/day, and a D1 database is single-threaded. Throughput is the real ceiling, not disk.
For scale: blocks cost nothing~200 bytes a row. A million blocks is 200 MB — 2% of the cap. Projection at 10,000 active users: ~250 MB after year one. Time Peek being client-side means watching your den tick never hits the database at all.

App Store Connect gets the custom domain for privacy and support URLs, not the github.io path — a .github.io/subpath privacy policy reads as less established, and the custom domain means you can move hosts without the links rotting.
